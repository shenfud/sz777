7 Free Resume Builders That Look Professionally Designed

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

You shouldn't pay $30 to make a resume as a student. These builders are genuinely free (or free enough), export clean PDFs without ugly watermarks, and won't trap your content behind a paywall halfway through.

1. Google Docs Templates

Best for: A clean, safe default that recruiters never complain about.

Not great for: Standing out visually.

Price: 100% free, exports PDF in one click.

Open Google Docs →

2. Canva

Best for: Designed templates for creative roles (marketing, design, media).

Not great for: Conservative industries like banking or law.

Price: Free templates and PDF export; students get extra assets free.

Try Canva →

3. FlowCV

Best for: Modern, minimal one-page resumes with free PDF export.

Not great for: Very complex multi-page CVs.

Price: Free core features.

Try FlowCV →

4. Novoresume

Best for: Guided content help — it nudges you to write better bullet points.

Not great for: Full template variety on the free tier.

Price: Free basic templates; premium unlocks the rest.

Try Novoresume →

5. Kickresume

Best for: Matching resume + cover letter templates in one place.

Not great for: Free downloads are limited to a few templates.

Price: Free tier available; paid for AI features.

Try Kickresume →

6. Rezi

Best for: ATS-friendly formatting (survives the resume-reading robots).

Not great for: Highly visual designs.

Price: Free tier with limited AI credits.

Try Rezi →

7. LinkedIn Profile

Best for: Not a builder — but recruiters will check it, so keep it consistent with your resume.

Not great for: Actually applying where a PDF is required.

Price: Free.

Polish your LinkedIn →

Our pick

Start with Google Docs or FlowCV for a classic layout, and keep a Canva version for creative roles. One page, no photo (unless local norms say otherwise), and always export as PDF.
