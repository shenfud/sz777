7 Free Tools to Make Presentations That Don't Look Terrible

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

A clean deck beats a clever one. You don't need a designer or a paid subscription to stop your slides from looking like a 2003 school project. Here are the free tools we'd actually use — for building, templating, and rescuing a deck the night before.

1. Google Slides

Best for: Everything. Free, cloud-based, auto-saves, and lets you collaborate in real time with classmates.

Not great for: Fancy animations — but you don't need those anyway.

Price: Free with a Google account.

Try Google Slides →

2. Canva

Best for: Making slides look designed without knowing design. Tons of free presentation templates.

Not great for: Heavy data charts — use Sheets/Excel for those.

Price: Free; students get extra assets.

Try Canva →

3. PowerPoint for the Web

Best for: People who already know PowerPoint but don't have the desktop app. The browser version is free and surprisingly complete.

Not great for: Offline work — it needs a connection.

Price: Free with a Microsoft account.

Open PowerPoint online →

4. Gamma

Best for: Turning a topic or bullet list into a full deck with AI in minutes. Great when you're starting from zero.

Not great for: Precise control over every pixel — it's fast, not fine-tuned.

Price: Free tier available.

Try Gamma →

5. Slidesgo

Best for: Free, good-looking templates you drop into Google Slides or PowerPoint. Saves hours of formatting.

Not great for: Totally original designs — you're working from a template (that's the point).

Price: Free templates with attribution.

See Slidesgo →

6. Prezi

Best for: Non-linear, zooming presentations that stand out in a sea of rectangular slides.

Not great for: Quick decks — the learning curve is real.

Price: Free basic plan.

Try Prezi →

7. Pitch

Best for: Modern, startup-style decks with clean templates and easy sharing via link.

Not great for: Printing — it's built for presenting on screen.

Price: Free plan available.

Try Pitch →

Our pick

For most students: Google Slides as your base, a Slidesgo template for looks, and Canva when you need a custom graphic. Only reach for Gamma if you're starting from a blank page at midnight.
