6 Free Budgeting Apps That Stop You Being Broke by Month-End

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

Mint shut down, and every "best budgeting app" list is now full of $99/year subscriptions. If you're a student, you don't need that. You need to know where your money went and whether you can order takeout tonight. These do that, for free.

1. Goodbudget

Best for: Envelope budgeting — split your money into categories before you spend it.

Not great for: Automatic bank syncing is limited.

Price: Free for a limited number of envelopes.

Try Goodbudget →

2. PocketGuard

Best for: One screen that answers "how much can I safely spend right now?"

Not great for: Deep category customization on the free tier.

Price: Free tier; paid adds more.

Try PocketGuard →

3. EveryDollar

Best for: Zero-based budgeting with a simple monthly layout.

Not great for: Auto-import requires the paid version.

Price: Free tier available.

Try EveryDollar →

4. Wally

Best for: Clean design and good multi-currency support.

Not great for: Some features are behind Wally+.

Price: Free core; optional premium.

Try Wally →

5. Google Sheets (Free Templates)

Best for: Total control, zero ads, and it never gets discontinued. Plenty of free student budget templates exist.

Not great for: You have to update it yourself.

Price: 100% free.

Find templates →

6. Your Bank's Built-In Tools

Best for: Already connected to your real money — many banks now include spending breakdowns and round-up savings for free.

Not great for: Cross-bank view if you have accounts in different places.

Price: Free with your account.

Check your banking app →

Our pick

Start with PocketGuard for the daily "can I afford this?" answer, or Goodbudget if you want to plan ahead. Too many apps fail because people over-engineer them — one app, five minutes a week, done.
