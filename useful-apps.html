10 Handy Apps You'll Actually Use Every Day in 2026

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

Not every app deserves a spot on your home screen. These 10 are the ones we keep coming back to — for getting organized, staying focused, splitting the bill, learning something new, and actually relaxing. Most are free or have a solid free tier.

1. Notion

Best for: Keeping your whole life in one place — notes, to-do lists, budgets, trip plans.

Not great for: People who want something dead simple.

Price: Free for individuals; students get the Plus plan free with a school email.

Try Notion →

2. Grammarly

Best for: Sending messages and emails that don't look sloppy.

Not great for: Replacing real proofreading of important documents.

Price: Free; Premium adds tone and clarity suggestions.

Try Grammarly →

3. Splitwise

Best for: Splitting rent, groceries, and trips with roommates and friends without the "who owes who" drama.

Not great for: Long-term shared savings goals.

Price: Free; Pro removes ads and adds receipts.

Try Splitwise →

4. Duolingo

Best for: Picking up a language in 5 minutes a day (yes, even just for travel).

Not great for: Reaching true fluency without talking to real people.

Price: Free; Super removes ads and adds offline lessons.

Try Duolingo →

5. Spotify

Best for: Music and podcasts while commuting, working out, or studying.

Not great for: Audiophiles who want lossless audio.

Price: Free with ads; the Student plan is heavily discounted (check in your country).

Check Spotify →

6. Canva

Best for: Making invites, resumes, posts, and slides when you have zero design skills.

Not great for: Print-press-quality or very complex layouts.

Price: Free; students get extra templates and assets free.

Try Canva →

7. Forest

Best for: Putting the phone down and actually finishing what you started.

Not great for: People who hate gamified focus tools.

Price: Small one-time app price; free trial often available.

Try Forest →

8. Todoist

Best for: A clean task list that doesn't make you feel behind before you start.

Not great for: Heavy project-management charts.

Price: Free; Pro adds reminders and calendar views.

Try Todoist →

9. Google Calendar

Best for: One shared schedule for classes, shifts, and plans across all your devices.

Not great for: Advanced scheduling logic.

Price: 100% free.

Learn more →

10. Headspace

Best for: Falling asleep faster and not spiraling at 2 a.m.

Not great for: People who want totally free meditation only.

Price: Free basics; Plus is a paid subscription (often discounted for students).

Try Headspace →

Our pick

If you only install a few: Notion (keep life together), Splitwise (stop the money fights), and Forest (actually get things done). Add Grammarly so your messages don't embarrass you. The rest are gravy.
