9 Free Online Course Platforms That Feel Like University (2026)

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

Tuition is ridiculous, but learning from the world's best universities isn't. These nine platforms let you take real courses from Harvard, MIT, and more — for free. The trick: most let you "audit" (take the class) at no cost, and only charge if you want a certificate.

1. Coursera (Audit for free)

Best for: University courses from 300+ schools; audit most for free, pay only for the certificate.

Watch out: Graded assignments are sometimes locked behind paid tracks.

Price: Free to audit; certificate paid (financial aid available).

Browse Coursera →

2. edX (Free university courses)

Best for: Harvard, MIT, and other top schools; the "audit track" is free and substantial.

Not great for: Certificates cost money if you want proof.

Price: Free audit; verified certificate paid.

Explore edX →

3. Khan Academy (Free, K-12 to early college)

Best for: Math, science, economics, and test prep explained simply and completely free.

Watch out: Not degree-level depth in every subject.

Price: Free, nonprofit.

Learn on Khan Academy →

4. MIT OpenCourseWare (Free, no login)

Best for: Full MIT course materials — lectures, exams, notes — published for free.

Watch out: No instructor, no grading, no certificate.

Price: Free.

Visit MIT OCW →

5. OpenLearn (Free, Open University)

Best for: Short, bite-sized courses with free statements of participation.

Not great for: Deep specializations.

Price: Free.

Study on OpenLearn →

6. FutureLearn (Free trial courses)

Best for: UK and global university courses with a friendly, social learning feel.

Watch out: Free access is time-limited unless you upgrade.

Price: Free tier; upgrades paid.

Join FutureLearn →

7. Alison (Free, certificates)

Best for: Practical, job-focused courses with free certificates (watch a few ads).

Watch out: Less prestige than university platforms.

Price: Free; certificate download may cost.

Take Alison courses →

8. Stanford Online & Yale (Free via Coursera/edX)

Best for: Famous courses like "CS50-style" intro series and "The Science of Well-Being," free to audit.

Watch out: Spread across platforms; search each school's site.

Price: Free to audit.

Find Stanford Online →

9. YouTube (Free, surprisingly good)

Best for: Supplementing any course — search a topic plus "lecture" and you'll find full playlists.

Not great for: Structure and accountability; pair it with a real course.

Price: Free.

Search course lectures →

Our pick

Audit Coursera or edX for structure, dip into MIT OCW for depth, and use Khan Academy to patch any gaps. You can build a self-taught education that rivals a degree — for the price of your time.
