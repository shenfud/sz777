12 Free Apps Every Student Should Use in 2026

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

School is expensive enough. These 12 apps are free (or have a solid free tier) and cover the things students actually struggle with: notes, writing, focus, and money. We use most of them ourselves.

1. Notion

Best for: Notes, planning, and group project tracking in one place.

Not great for: Quick handwritten math.

Price: Free for individuals; students get the Plus plan free with a school email.

Try Notion →

2. Grammarly

Best for: Catching grammar and clarity issues in essays.

Not great for: Deep academic citation rules.

Price: Free; Premium has a student discount.

Try Grammarly →

3. Khan Academy

Best for: Free lessons in math, science, and economics.

Not great for: Very advanced or niche university courses.

Price: 100% free.

Visit Khan Academy →

4. Quizlet

Best for: Flashcards and quick memorization.

Not great for: Long-form understanding.

Price: Free; Plus adds offline and extra modes.

Try Quizlet →

5. Canva

Best for: Slides, posters, and social graphics without design skills.

Not great for: Print-press-quality work.

Price: Free; students get extra templates free.

Try Canva →

6. Libby

Best for: Free ebooks and audiobooks with a library card.

Not great for: Brand-new bestsellers (waitlists).

Price: Free with a library card.

Get Libby →

7. Spotify (Student)

Best for: Music and podcasts while studying.

Not great for: Hi-fi audio fans.

Price: Student plan is heavily discounted (verify in your country).

Check Spotify Student →

8. Google Workspace for Education

Best for: Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Drive with your school account.

Not great for: Advanced desktop features.

Price: Free for eligible students.

Learn more →

9. Duolingo

Best for: Learning a language in 5-minute daily bites.

Not great for: Fluency without real conversation.

Price: Free; Super removes ads.

Try Duolingo →

10. Microsoft 365 (Education)

Best for: Word, Excel, and PowerPoint many courses require.

Not great for: Heavy offline use without install.

Price: Free for eligible students.

Check eligibility →

11. Todoist

Best for: Simple task lists that don't overwhelm.

Not great for: Complex project charts.

Price: Free; Pro adds reminders and views.

Try Todoist →

12. Forest

Best for: Staying off your phone while studying.

Not great for: People who hate gamified focus.

Price: Small one-time app price; free trial often available.

Try Forest →

Our pick

Start with Notion (organize), Grammarly (writing), and Forest (focus). Those three cover most of the daily struggle. Add Libby and you stop paying for books and audiobooks.
