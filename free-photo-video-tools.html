7 Free Photo & Video Editing Tools That Don't Suck (2026)

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

Adobe's subscription is a luxury you don't need in 2026. Whether you're fixing a profile pic or cutting a YouTube video, these free tools get the job done. Every one below has a real free tier — no "free trial that expires in 7 days" tricks.

1. Canva (Free, all-rounder)

Best for: Quick social posts, thumbnails, and simple photo edits with drag-and-drop templates.

Watch out: Advanced features sit behind Pro; the free tier covers most needs.

Price: Free tier is generous.

Use Canva Free →

2. GIMP (Free, Photoshop alternative)

Best for: Serious photo retouching and layers without paying for Photoshop.

Not great for: The interface feels dated and the learning curve is steep.

Price: 100% free, open source.

Download GIMP →

3. DaVinci Resolve (Free, pro video)

Best for: Editing videos with Hollywood-grade color and cutting tools — the free version is shockingly capable.

Watch out: Heavy on older laptops; needs a decent GPU.

Price: Free version is full-featured.

Get DaVinci Resolve →

4. CapCut (Free, short-form video)

Best for: TikTok, Reels, and Shorts — auto-captions, trendy templates, and one-tap edits.

Watch out: Some trending assets are watermarked unless you're on Pro.

Price: Free core app.

Edit with CapCut →

5. Photopea (Free, runs in browser)

Best for: Opening and editing PSD files without Photoshop — works entirely in your browser.

Watch out: Ad-supported; an offline app feels snappier for big files.

Price: Free.

Open Photopea →

6. Pixlr (Free, lightweight editor)

Best for: Fast browser-based edits and a Photoshop-like feel without installing anything.

Not great for: Complex multi-layer compositing.

Price: Free tier available.

Try Pixlr →

7. Lightworks (Free, timeline editing)

Best for: Traditional timeline video editing if DaVinci feels like overkill.

Watch out: Free export is limited to 720p; fine for web.

Price: Free version.

Use Lightworks →

Our pick

For photos, start with Canva (easiest) and keep Photopea bookmarked for PSD files. For video, CapCut covers short-form and DaVinci Resolve handles anything bigger. You can run a whole content side-hustle on $0 here.
