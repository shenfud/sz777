8 Free Places to Learn Coding in 2026 (No Degree Required)

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

You don't need a $40,000 bootcamp or a computer-science degree to learn to code. These eight resources are genuinely free and good enough to take you from "hello world" to a portfolio that gets interviews. Pick one and finish it — consistency beats collecting tutorials.

1. freeCodeCamp (Free, project-based)

Best for: Learning by building — you earn certificates by completing real projects in HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Python, and more.

Watch out: It's self-paced, so you have to motivate yourself.

Price: 100% free, no account paywall.

Start freeCodeCamp →

2. The Odin Project (Free, full-stack path)

Best for: People who want a clear route to becoming a web developer, with a curated path instead of random videos.

Not great for: Casual dabblers — it expects commitment.

Price: Free.

Explore The Odin Project →

3. CS50 by Harvard (Free, university quality)

Best for: A real Harvard intro-to-computer-science course, with English subtitles and a famous final project.

Watch out: It's challenging — pace yourself if you're brand new.

Price: Free to audit on edX; certificate is optional paid.

Watch CS50 →

4. Codecademy (Free tier)

Best for: Absolute beginners who like interactive, in-browser lessons with instant feedback.

Watch out: The best paths are behind a paid Pro plan; the free tier is enough to start.

Price: Free tier available; Pro is optional.

Try Codecademy Free →

5. Khan Academy (Free, gentle start)

Best for: Younger students or total newcomers who want a friendly, no-pressure intro to programming and computer science.

Not great for: Job-ready depth — pair it with a project-based resource.

Price: Free.

Learn on Khan Academy →

6. MDN Web Docs (Free reference)

Best for: The go-to documentation once you know the basics — clear explanations of HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

Watch out: It's a reference, not a course; learn syntax elsewhere first.

Price: Free.

Open MDN Web Docs →

7. MIT OpenCourseWare (Free, deep)

Best for: Ambitious learners who want full university-level CS lectures and problem sets, for free.

Watch out: No hand-holding and no grading.

Price: Free.

Browse MIT OCW →

8. W3Schools (Free, quick lookups)

Best for: Fast, simple examples you can copy while building — great as a companion, not a main course.

Not great for: Big-picture learning; it's bite-sized.

Price: Free (some certificates paid).

Use W3Schools →

Our pick

Start with freeCodeCamp or The Odin Project for structure, keep MDN open in a second tab as your dictionary, and watch CS50 when you want to understand why things work. Finish one path before jumping to the next.
