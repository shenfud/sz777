9 Free Ways to Learn a Language (Without Paying for a Subscription)

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

Language apps love to show you one lesson free, then slam the paywall. You can get surprisingly far without ever paying — if you stack the right free tools. Here are nine that build real skill, not just streaks.

1. Duolingo

Best for: Daily habit-building and basics in 30+ languages. The free tier is enough to make real progress.

Not great for: Deep grammar or speaking fluency — it's a starting point.

Price: Free; Super is optional.

Try Duolingo →

2. Language Transfer

Best for: Understanding how a language actually works, through free audio lessons. Brilliant for Spanish, Greek, and more.

Not great for: Reading and writing practice — it's audio-only.

Price: 100% free, donation-supported.

Try Language Transfer →

3. Anki (Flashcards)

Best for: Remembering vocabulary long-term with spaced repetition. Free on desktop and Android.

Not great for: Beginners who need explanations — it's raw memorization.

Price: Free (iOS version is paid, but the web is free).

Try Anki →

4. HelloTalk / Tandem

Best for: Free language exchange — chat with native speakers who want to learn your language. Real conversation, zero cost.

Not great for: Structured lessons; it's social, not a course.

Price: Free; premium optional.

Try HelloTalk →

5. YouTube

Best for: Free lessons and immersion — search "[language] for beginners" and follow a channel you like.

Not great for: Speaking practice; it's mostly input.

Price: Free.

Find lessons →

6. Podcasts & Radio

Best for: Passive listening while walking or doing chores — trains your ear to real-speed speech.

Not great for: Beginners who need visuals; start with "slow news" podcasts.

Price: Free (Spotify, Apple Podcasts, etc.).

Browse podcasts →

7. Clozemaster

Best for: Learning words in context (fill-in-the-blank sentences) once you know the basics. Feels like a game.

Not great for: Absolute beginners.

Price: Free tier.

Try Clozemaster →

8. Memrise

Best for: Real-native video clips and memory tricks for everyday phrases. The free tier covers a lot.

Not great for: Grammar depth.

Price: Free; Pro optional.

Try Memrise →

9. Free eBooks + a Dictionary App

Best for: Reading real material at your level — Project Gutenberg has free public-domain books in many languages.

Not great for: Listening practice.

Price: Free (Gutenberg + any free dictionary).

Our pick

Build a stack: Duolingo for daily basics, Language Transfer to actually understand the logic, Anki for vocab, and HelloTalk to finally speak with a real person. Add podcasts during downtime and you've got a full, free curriculum.
