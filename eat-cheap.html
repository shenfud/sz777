9 Free Apps & Hacks to Eat Well on a Student Budget

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

"Broke" doesn't have to mean "unfed" or "only ramen." These nine free tools and habits cut your food bill and make the cheap stuff actually taste good. A few are apps; the rest are habits that pay off every single week.

1. Too Good To Go

Why: Buy "surprise bags" of unsold food from local shops and restaurants at a steep discount — often a third of the price.

Watch out: It's random; go in flexible about what you get.

Price: Free app; you pay only for the bags.

Try Too Good To Go →

2. OLIO

Why: Free food shared by neighbors — someone's leftover lasagna or spare veg, listed near you. Zero cost.

Watch out: Availability depends on your area.

Price: Free.

Try OLIO →

3. Flashfood

Why: Discounts on near-expiry groceries at partner stores — same food, much lower price.

Watch out: Only works with certain supermarkets; check if yours is listed.

Price: Free app; pay for discounted items.

Try Flashfood →

4. SuperCook

Best for: Type what's already in your fridge and get recipes — stops you buying more just to use what you have.

Not great for: Fancy cooking; it's practical, not gourmet.

Price: Free.

Try SuperCook →

5. Mealime

Best for: Free meal plans and shopping lists built around your diet and how many people you cook for.

Not great for: Very niche diets on the free tier.

Price: Free; Pro optional.

Try Mealime →

6. YouTube Cooking Channels

Why: Free, visual, and better than any text recipe when you're learning. Search "student meal prep" and pick a style.

Watch out: Easy to fall into a watch-hole — cook, don't just watch.

Price: Free.

Find recipes →

7. Store Loyalty Apps

Why: Your regular supermarket's free app usually has digital coupons and points you're leaving on the table.

Watch out: Only worth it if you shop there anyway — don't drive across town for points.

Price: Free.

Check your store's app →

8. The "Generic Brand" Habit

Why: Store brands are often 30–50% cheaper and nearly identical for staples — rice, pasta, oats, canned goods.

Watch out: A few things (chocolate, coffee) are worth the name brand.

Price: Free habit, real savings.

Shop smart →

9. Batch-Cook Sundays

Why: Cook once, eat four times. One hour Sunday saves you from $12 delivery orders all week.

Watch out: You'll eat the same thing a few days — that's the trade-off.

Price: Free; saves the most money of anything here.

Get started →

Our pick

Start with the habit, not the apps: batch-cook and buy generic. Then add Too Good To Go for cheap surprise meals and SuperCook so nothing in your fridge goes to waste. That combo easily saves $30–50 a week.
