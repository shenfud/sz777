10 Affordable Sleep Aids for Better Rest on a Student Budget

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

Bad sleep is the silent killer of grades, mood, and every group project. You don't need a $300 mattress or a sleep clinic — these ten cheap fixes cover the three things that actually wreck student sleep: light, noise, and a racing mind. Most are under $20.

1. Foam Earplugs (Physical)

Why: A roommate's 2 a.m. typing or a thin dorm wall will never match your schedule. Earplugs are the single cheapest sleep upgrade that exists.

Watch out: Sizes vary — grab a sampler pack to find your fit.

Price: Around $8–12 for a big box.

See earplugs →

2. Blackout Eye Mask (Physical)

Why: Stops streetlights, hallway lights, and that one friend who leaves the lamp on. Pairs perfectly with earplugs.

Watch out: Pick a contoured one so it doesn't press on your eyelids.

Price: Around $10–15.

See sleep masks →

3. White-Noise / Ambient App (Free)

Best for: Falling asleep to rain, fans, or brown noise when you can't control the real environment. myNoise and the free tier of BetterSleep are solid.

Not great for: People who need total silence — this is the opposite.

Price: Free (paid tiers optional).

Try a noise app →

4. Blue-Light Blocking Glasses (Physical)

Why: Late-night screen time tricks your brain into staying awake. Amber lenses in the hour before bed help.

Watch out: Cheap pairs work fine; you don't need the fancy ones.

Price: Around $15–25.

See blue-light glasses →

5. Blackout Curtains or Window Film (Physical)

Why: Sunrise at 5 a.m. during finals is brutal. Static-cling window film needs no tools and leaves no damage deposit.

Watch out: Measure your window before ordering.

Price: Around $12–20.

See blackout film →

6. Sleep-Tracker App (Free)

Best for: Figuring out why you're tired — Sleep Cycle's free tier tracks sleep and wakes you in a light phase.

Not great for: Medical accuracy; it's a guide, not a diagnosis.

Price: Free.

Try a sleep app →

7. Herbal Tea or Magnesium Drink (Physical)

Why: A warm, caffeine-free routine signals your body it's bedtime. Chamomile or a low-cost magnesium mix both work.

Watch out: Check with a doctor if you take other meds.

Price: Around $6–10.

See sleep tea →

8. Cooling Pillow or Pillow Spray (Physical)

Why: A hot, stuffy pillow is a common reason people wake up. A cooling pillow or a lavender spray is a cheap fix.

Watch out: Spray is the cheaper test before buying a whole pillow.

Price: Around $10–18.

See pillow spray →

9. Phone Bedtime Mode (Free)

Best for: Killing notifications and dimming the screen automatically. Built into iOS and Android — zero cost, zero effort.

Not great for: You still have to actually put the phone down.

Price: Free (already on your phone).

Set it up →

10. Weighted Blanket (Budget Pick)

Why: The "hug" feeling genuinely lowers restlessness for many people. Get a smaller, lighter, cheaper one for a twin bed.

Watch out: The full-size ones are pricey and heavy — a lap-size one does the job for less.

Price: Around $30–45 for a small one.

See weighted blankets →

Our pick

Start with the free stuff: turn on Bedtime Mode, download a white-noise app, and set a tea routine. Then spend about $20 total on earplugs + eye mask — that combo alone solves most student sleep problems. Everything else is a nice-to-have.
