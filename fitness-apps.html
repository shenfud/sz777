8 Free Workout & Fitness Apps for Broke Students

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

A gym membership is the first thing to cut when money's tight — but fitness doesn't have to go with it. These eight free apps cover strength, cardio, yoga, and food, all without a single monthly fee. No equipment needed for most of them.

1. Nike Training Club

Best for: Free, well-made workout plans — strength, HIIT, yoga, and mobility — with clear video demos.

Not great for: Tracking runs; pair it with a running app for that.

Price: Free.

Try Nike Training Club →

2. FitOn

Best for: Studio-style classes (HIIT, Pilates, dance) led by trainers, all free. Feels premium without the price.

Not great for: Heavy strength programming.

Price: Free; optional Pro tier.

Try FitOn →

3. Home Workout (No Equipment)

Best for: Quick bodyweight sessions in a dorm room — push-ups, squats, planks, with a simple rep counter.

Not great for: People who need music and production value to stay motivated.

Price: Free (ad-supported).

Try Home Workout →

4. Strava

Best for: Logging runs, rides, and walks, plus a free community that keeps you accountable. The free tier is plenty.

Not great for: Guided workouts (those are mostly paid now).

Price: Free tier.

Try Strava →

5. MyFitnessPal

Best for: Free food logging and a huge database — handy if your goal is weight, not just exercise.

Not great for: The free version has limits on some insights.

Price: Free; Premium optional.

Try MyFitnessPal →

6. Yoga with Adriene (YouTube)

Best for: Free, friendly yoga for every level — the most-watched yoga channel for a reason. Zero app needed.

Not great for: Structured progression; it's a playlist, not a program.

Price: Free on YouTube.

Watch on YouTube →

7. The 7-Minute Workout

Best for: Days when you have nothing in you — a science-backed circuit you can finish between classes.

Not great for: Building serious muscle; it's maintenance, not gains.

Price: Free.

Try 7-Minute Workout →

8. YouTube (Free Workout Channels)

Best for: Basically every style for free — search "no equipment workout" and pick a face you like. Endless and free.

Not great for: Structure; you have to self-motivate.

Price: Free.

Browse workouts →

Our pick

Pair Nike Training Club (or FitOn) for workouts with Strava for cardio and MyFitnessPal if you care about food. That trio is a complete, completely free fitness setup.
