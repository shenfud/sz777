7 Legit Ways to Get Textbooks for (Almost) Free

Updated for 2026 · Free first, cheap second

Textbooks can cost more than the course sometimes. Before you click "buy new" in the campus store, run through this list — in order. Most students who spend $300+ a semester simply skipped step 1.

1. OpenStax (Open Textbooks)

Why: Free, peer-reviewed textbooks for intro math, science, economics, and more — many professors build courses around them.

Watch out: Mostly intro-level subjects.

Browse OpenStax →

2. Your Campus Library (Course Reserves)

Why: Required books are often held at the desk for short-term borrowing — free, first-come-first-served.

Watch out: Popular titles go fast before exams.

Check your library →

3. Libby (with a Library Card)

Why: Free ebooks and audiobooks, including many popular editions, through your local library.

Watch out: Waitlists on new titles.

Get Libby →

4. Internet Archive / Open Library

Why: Millions of digitized books, borrowable for free — surprisingly good for older editions.

Watch out: Availability varies by title.

Browse Open Library →

5. Ask for the Older Edition

Why: The "new edition" usually shuffles exercises, not content. An older edition at $10 covers 95% of the course.

Watch out: Check with the professor if problem numbers matter.

Compare editions →

6. Rent Instead of Buying

Why: Renting typically costs a third of the new price — and you're never reading it again after finals anyway.

Watch out: Highlighting rules and return deadlines.

Compare rentals →

7. Split a Copy with a Classmate

Why: For courses where the book is reference-only, splitting the cost 2–3 ways works fine.

Watch out: Only for books you won't both need nightly.

Find cheaper copies →

Our pick

Before any purchase: library reserves → OpenStax → older edition → rent. That chain alone cuts a typical semester's book bill by more than half.
